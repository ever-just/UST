## 1. IP and Legal Landscape

### 1.1 Applicable IP to Kinetic’s Products
- **Q1**: What specific types of IP protection are relevant to FlexiStep and KneeComfort Pro?
  - Utility patents (functions, methods, sensor integration?).
  - Design patents (overall look/shape, contours, geometry?).
  - Trademarks/trade dress (branding, overall appearance).
  - Copyright (marketing materials, manuals, app UI).
- **Research tasks**:
  - Identify typical IP protection used in orthopedic devices and foam positioning products.
  - Clarify which categories are most likely implicated by Kinetic’s designs.

### 1.2 Competitors’ IP Positions (Stryker & BoneFoam)
- **Q2**: What is Stryker’s and BoneFoam’s current IP portfolio that is **relevant** to Kinetic’s products?
  - Key patents on Infinity Total Ankle System (Stryker).
  - Key patents on BoneFoam’s foam positioners (shapes, angles, materials).
- **Q3–4**: What is the **likelihood of dispute** from each company?
  - What is their **legal conflict history** (how litigious are they)?
  - How many companies have they pursued for infringement, and in what circumstances?
- **Q19**: For BoneFoam specifically, what is their **history of enforcing IP**? Do they aggressively pursue other companies?
- **Research tasks**:
  - Look up litigation history, cease‑and‑desist reports, or notable IP cases involving Stryker and BoneFoam.
  - Find sample cases that show when courts treat “similar” medical devices as infringing.

### 1.3 HIPAA, Privacy, and Data Misuse Risk
- **Q12**: Are there **existing HIPAA violations** or enforcement actions involving:
  - Doctors or institutions sharing patient data with device companies.
  - Tech/health companies building datasets from patient‑provided data or websites/apps?
- **Q13**: What are the main **risks of data leaks** when using a website or app for patient data upload?
  - What security controls are expected.
  - What incident response plans are expected if data leaks occur.
- **Research tasks**:
  - Find examples of HIPAA enforcement cases involving data sharing with commercial entities.
  - Identify best practices for securing health data collected via consumer‑facing apps/websites.

## 2. Product Uniqueness, Effectiveness, and Value Proposition

### 2.1 Uniqueness of Kinetic’s Solutions
- **Q5**: How unique are FlexiStep and KneeComfort Pro compared to existing competitor solutions?
  - Have competitors already embraced similar **value propositions** or features?
- **Q14 (part 1)**: Is there scope to **differentiate the design** visibly?
  - Colors, materials, aesthetics, adjustability, user experience.
- **Research tasks**:
  - Map specific features of FlexiStep/KneeComfort Pro to Stryker/BoneFoam and other competitors.
  - Identify aspects that can be safely differentiated without increasing IP risk.

### 2.2 Effectiveness Evidence
- **Q6 & Q10 & Q20**: How **effective** are current products offered by Stryker and BoneFoam?
  - Are there white papers, clinical trials, or research papers showing effectiveness in improving recovery outcomes (ROM, pain scores, time to recovery)?
- **Research tasks**:
  - Search for clinical studies, white papers, or case studies involving Infinity Total Ankle System and BoneFoam positioning products.
  - Extract outcome metrics that Kinetic could aim to meet or exceed (without copying design).

### 2.3 Technology / Tracking App Differentiator
- **Q7**: If Kinetic uses a **recovery‑tracking app**, do competitors (Stryker, BoneFoam, others) already offer similar apps or digital solutions?
- **Q9**: Can Kinetic legitimately position the app as a **unique selling point** if competitors lack similar tracking capabilities?
- **Research tasks**:
  - Survey existing digital health tools tied to orthopedic devices (post‑op tracking, remote monitoring).
  - Identify gaps that Kinetic could ethically fill.

## 3. Data Strategy, Consent, and Ethics

### 3.1 Website/App Data Collection
- **Q8**: If patients use a **website to upload their own recovery data**, does this reduce friction around consent compared to requesting data from physicians?
  - Does it meaningfully clarify that data is for **Kinetic’s products**, not competitors’?
- **Q13 (re‑emphasis)**: Ethical and legal concerns about a website/app for patient data upload:
  - Likelihood and consequences of data leaks.
  - How to prevent them and how to respond if they occur.
- **Research tasks**:
  - Compare legal/ethical issues of physician‑mediated data vs. patient‑entered data.
  - Identify good models for consent language and user flows in health apps.

### 3.2 HIPAA and Analogous Cases
- **Q12 (re‑emphasis)**: Case studies where:
  - Healthcare entities or tech companies mishandled patient‑contributed data.
  - HIPAA or other privacy law violations occurred for similar data uses.
- **Research tasks**:
  - Gather precedent that shows the **line between acceptable and unacceptable** data use models.

## 4. Market, Customers, and Go‑To‑Market Strategy

### 4.1 Core Customer Segments
- **Q15**: Who **primarily** suffers the types of injuries these products address?
  - Athletes? Older adults? Workers with repetitive strain? Post‑surgical patients generally?
- **Q26/Q14 (part 2)**: Is there a **core customer segment** Kinetic can strongly appeal to?
  - Is there opportunity to leverage **geography** (Minnesota / Midwest / local sports culture)?
  - Any other unique selling points (startup agility, patient‑centric tech, cost)?
- **Research tasks**:
  - Identify epidemiology and demographics of ankle/foot and knee‑related injuries requiring these kinds of supports.
  - Look for segment‑specific needs (e.g., athletes needing return‑to‑sport metrics vs. older adults prioritizing fall prevention).

### 4.2 Targeting and Customization
- **Q16**: Based on the primary segment, how can Kinetic **target that audience** better?
  - For athletes (e.g., hockey players): partnerships with teams, leagues, training facilities.
  - For older adults: partnerships with PT clinics, home health, Medicare‑focused providers.
- **Q11**: How to overcome existing doctors’ **consulting agreements or loyalty** to incumbents?
  - Should Kinetic consider a strategy similar to pharma DTC advertising: empowering patients to ask doctors about the product?
- **Research tasks**:
  - Explore ethical and regulatory boundaries of DTC marketing for medical devices.
  - Look at examples of successful and controversial DTC campaigns.

### 4.3 Route to Market (Physician‑Mediated vs. Direct‑to‑Consumer)
- **Q17**: Clarify product type differences:
  - Stryker’s Infinity is an **implant**; FlexiStep is **not** an implant.
  - Are Kinetic’s products primarily for **post‑operative recovery**?
  - Does Stryker offer **non‑implant** products in the same recovery segment?
- **Q18**: If products are for **post‑recovery**, do they **require physician prescription**, or could they be partly **direct‑to‑consumer**?
- **Research tasks**:
  - Determine regulatory and practical expectations for distribution channels of similar braces and foam supports.
  - Identify pros/cons and risks of going DTC vs. physician‑prescribed.
